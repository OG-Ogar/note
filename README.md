	1. Silence is louder than words.
	
	Neuroscience shows people who pause before speaking are perceived as more intelligent. Every “umm” bleeds authority.
	
	Wait. Breathe. Then drop your words like they matter.
	2. Don’t chase — let gravity work.
	
	Scarcity bias: what’s rare is valuable.
	
	Stop over-explaining, stop over-texting. Pull back, and people lean in.
	3. Mirror, don’t mimic.
	
	Psychologists call it the chameleon effect.
	
	Subtle mirroring builds instant trust. But be slick about it — no one likes a wannabe parrot.
	4. Control your face, control the room.
	
	Microexpressions leak weakness. Poker players know this.
	
	Next time you’re stressed, soften your eyes, relax your jaw. Calm spreads like a virus.
	
	5. The first frame wins.
	
	Social psychology: whoever sets the context, sets the power dynamic.
	
	Walk into the room like it’s your movie. People will unconsciously follow your script.
	6. Ask questions that flip the script.
	
	Instead of “Do you like it?” say: “What would you change about it?”
	
	You’ve just positioned yourself as evaluator, not applicant.
	7. Small talk is a trap — go deep fast.
	
	Neuroscience of bonding: deep conversation releases oxytocin (the trust hormone). Forget the weather.
	
	Ask: “What’s lighting you up this week?”
	8. Don’t explain — state.
	
	Power is declarative. Weakness is apologetic.
	
	Compare: “Sorry, I think maybe we should…” vs. “Here’s the play.” Feel the shift?
	9. Mystery > Transparency.
	
	People overvalue what they don’t fully understand.
	
	A little unpredictability makes you magnetic. Think James Bond, not an open diary.
	10. Use names like weapons.
	
	Hearing your own name lights up your brain like fireworks (fMRI proven).
	
	Sprinkle names in conversation — they’ll lean closer.
	11. Stay emotionally unbothered.
	
	Stoics were right: anger is free power you hand over.
	
	Respond slow, flat, unshaken. Nothing rattles people more than your calm.
	12. Interrupt the rhythm.
	
	Drop your voice to a whisper mid-sentence. Pause longer than usual.
	
	People can’t look away from disrupted patterns.
	13. Don’t defend — redirect.
	
	In arguments, defense = weakness.
	
	Instead, pivot: “That’s interesting. Let’s test it.” You’ve just moved from prey to referee.
	14. Status lives in body language.
	
	Psych studies: expansive postures = dominance.
	
	Shrinking postures = submission. Shoulders back. Chest open. Eye contact steady. Instant shift.
	15. End on a cliffhanger.
	
	Zeigarnik effect: unfinished stories haunt the brain.
	
	Don’t overshare. Leave them wanting. Curiosity is addictive.
	16. Own your shadow.
	
	Carl Jung said: what you repress controls you. Admit your flaws.
	
	Laugh at them. People can’t use what you’ve already owned against you.
	Finally
	
	Power isn’t about being manipulative. It’s about mastering the psychology of how humans actually work.
